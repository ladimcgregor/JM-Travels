The Schengen visa process is manageable once you know what's being asked of you, but a surprising number of applications get delayed over documentation issues that are easy to avoid. Here's what to have ready before you book an appointment.

## Core documents

- Valid passport with at least two blank pages and validity extending well beyond your return date
- Completed and signed application form
- Recent passport-style photographs meeting the specific size and background requirements
- Proof of travel insurance meeting the minimum coverage requirement
- Flight reservation (not necessarily a paid ticket, but a confirmed itinerary)
- Proof of accommodation for the full length of stay
- Financial evidence: recent bank statements are the most common request

## Where applications commonly run into problems

- **Inconsistent dates** between the flight itinerary, accommodation booking and the application form
- **Insufficient financial evidence**, particularly for longer stays
- **Missing cover letter** explaining the purpose and structure of the trip, especially for group or multi-country itineraries
- **Booking non-refundable flights or hotels before the visa is approved**: most consulates don't require this, and it creates unnecessary financial risk if the application is refused

## A note on outcomes

No travel consultant or visa assistance service can guarantee approval; the decision sits solely with the relevant embassy or consulate. What a structured process can do is make sure your application is complete, consistent and presented properly, which materially reduces the chance of an avoidable delay or refusal.
